How it learns

This library has one job: find out which first messages land, and keep improving them.

What counts as landing

Took the action within 14 days is the headline: they turned on auto recharge, replied with who owns access, shipped the first deploy. Replied is the early read while action data is thin.

The loop

  1. Each play has one first message with a version ID, like 01-A.
  2. Send it, then log it below with the version ID.
  3. After 7 days, log the outcome: no reply, replied, or took the action.
  4. Every week, compare versions. After about 10 sends, keep the winner and write a version B to beat it.

Built with the 5-step algorithm: question the requirements, delete, simplify, speed up, automate last. Results are counted by hand until the format earns automation.

Play 01At risk

Credit cliff: Pro account will run out mid-cycle, auto recharge off

Trigger

Pro account past 75% of monthly credits with more than a week left in the cycle, and auto recharge off.

Why it matters

When a Pro or Personal account runs out of credits, every site pauses and shows "Site not available." No new deploys. Forms stop taking submissions [Public, docs]. Netlify emails at 50%, 75% and 100% [Public, docs], but who gets those emails is a week-one question [Confirm]. For a small business, a paused site is lost sales today and a churn reason at renewal.

The fix, in plain words

Turn on auto recharge (1,500 credits for $10 on Pro), or move to a bigger monthly credit tier (Pro offers 5,000 up to 20,000) [Public, Nov 19, 2026].

Sample account (made up)

Juniper & Rye Bakery. Pro plan, 3 members. Day 20 of 30, at 2,640 of 3,000 credits (88%). Auto recharge off. Bandwidth is 70% of usage after a holiday menu post went viral.

First message goes to

The Team Owner, because only they can turn on auto recharge. Copy the user so the fix doesn't wait on a handoff.

First message · Version 01-A · to the Team Owner (renewal signer)
Subject: Your bakery site will pause in about 3 days Hi {first name}, Your Netlify account has used 2,640 of its 3,000 monthly credits, and the month resets in 10 days. At this pace you run out in about 3. When that happens your site shows "Site not available" and online orders stop. The cause is good news. Your holiday menu post is drawing a lot of traffic. Two ways to keep the site up: 1. Turn on auto recharge. It adds 1,500 credits for $10 only when you need them. 2. Move to a 5,000 credit plan if this traffic is the new normal. Option 1 takes a minute and only a Team Owner can do it. Here's where: {link to billing settings}. If option 2 sounds right, let me know and we'll find 15 minutes to size the plan to your traffic. Mike

Hand off when

Traffic looks like bots or a bug: Support. They want more than Pro offers: Sales [Confirm the threshold].

Success looks like

Auto recharge on or plan changed before 100%. Site never pauses.

Play 02Watch

Gone quiet: deploys dropped and renewal is inside 90 days

Trigger

Production deploys over the last 4 weeks are less than half of the 4-month weekly average, and the renewal date is within 90 days.

Why it matters

Fewer deploys can mean two things: the site is finished and stable, or the team is moving somewhere else. The data can't tell you which. A question can. Asking early beats the "do you want to renew?" email that arrives with no history [Said, Matt].

Sample account (made up)

Northfield Physical Therapy. $6K per year contract. Averaged 9 production deploys a week since July, 2 a week for the last 4. Renewal in 71 days. One developer, an office manager who pays the bill.

First message goes to

The user. They know why deploys dropped. Don't put renewal in front of the signer until you know.

First message · Version 02-A · to the user (developer)
Subject: Is the site where you want it? Hi {first name}, I look after your Netlify account. Your team shipped about 9 updates a week through the fall. The last month it's been about 2. That's often a good sign: the site is done and stable. Sometimes it means something got in the way. Which one is it for you? One line is plenty. Mike

Hand off when

The user names a technical blocker: Support. They mention moving to Vercel or Cloudflare: flag to Matt.

Success looks like

You know which kind of quiet it is within 14 days, and the signer hears from you before the renewal notice.

Play 03Ready to grow

Team outgrew its login: members jumped, security questions started

Trigger

Team members doubled in 60 days, or a support ticket mentions SSO, security review, offboarding or a compliance questionnaire.

Why it matters

SSO is the moment a team tool becomes a company-approved tool, and the buyer is usually IT or security, not the developer [Said, Matt]. It's the first rung on the expansion ladder. Publicly, SSO is listed under Enterprise [Public]. How a Tech Touch account gets it is a week-one question [Confirm].

Sample account (made up)

Ledgerly, a 40-person bookkeeping firm. Pro plan. Members went from 4 to 11 in six weeks. Last week someone asked Support how to remove a former contractor's access.

First message goes to

The champion. They can tell you who in IT or ops owns access, and they want to be the one who brings the fix.

First message · Version 03-A · to the champion
Subject: Access for a bigger team Hi {first name}, Your Netlify team went from 4 people to 11 since October, and someone asked us last week how to remove a former contractor. That usually means it's time for single sign-on, so access follows your company login and offboarding happens in one place. Who owns logins and access at Ledgerly? I'm happy to send them the details or join a short call with you both. Mike

Hand off when

The buyer says yes to the walkthrough: Sales, with notes on who's who [Confirm what a CSA can quote or close].

Success looks like

You have the buyer's name and a meeting booked, and the champion got the credit.

Play 04At risk

Stuck at first deploy: project created, nothing live a week later

Trigger

A new project with failed deploys, or no production deploy 7 days after signup.

Why it matters

More and more new customers built their site with an AI tool and have never seen a build setting [Public, Netlify's AX pitch]. When the first deploy fails or shows a blank page, they rarely file a ticket. They just never come back [Inference]. The build settings page assumes you know what a build is. A brand new user may not [Mike's own first deploy, Nov 20, 2026]. Failed deploys don't cost credits [Public, docs], so this account looks quiet and cheap, not broken.

The fix, in plain words

Usually one setting. A finished site (plain HTML) needs no build command. A site made with a framework or AI tool usually needs a build command like npm run build and a publish folder like dist. A wrong publish folder is a common cause of a blank page or "Page not found" [Inference, confirm with Support].

Sample account (made up)

Saltbox Yoga. Personal plan, 1 member, signed up 9 days ago. 3 failed deploys, 0 live. The owner built the site with an AI tool and is also the one paying.

First message goes to

The person who built it. They're stuck and they're the only one who can fix it. On a one-person account the builder, champion and signer are the same person, so this is the one time it looks like Future.

First message · Version 04-A · to the user (the builder)
Subject: Your site isn't live yet Hi {first name}, I look after new Netlify accounts. Your project has tried to go live 3 times and hasn't made it yet. That's almost always one setting, not something wrong with your site. Can you reply with how you built it (for example Bolt, Lovable, or plain HTML)? I'll send the exact setting to change. Mike

Hand off when

They reply with an error message or a build log: Support. Don't guess at code.

Success looks like

First production deploy within 7 days of your message.

Log a send

Log each send twice: once on the day you send it (outcome "Sent, waiting"), and again after 7 days with what happened.

Weekly review (15 minutes)

  1. In Netlify, open Forms, then send-log, and download the submissions.
  2. For each version, count sends, replies and actions. Use only the 7-day entries.
  3. Under about 10 sends, don't call a winner. Read the notes instead.
  4. Over 10 sends, write a version B for the weakest message and change one thing only.
